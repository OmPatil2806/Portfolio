FinCast

Forecasts next month's income, spending and balance, with a range instead of a single guess.

  • 6.9% lower MAE than best baseline
  • 85% of actuals inside the range
  • 289 tests, 92% coverage
View on GitHub

Problem

A single predicted number for next month is almost always wrong and gives no sense of how wrong. FinCast forecasts a range — 10 to 90% quantiles — and is judged on whether the real value actually lands inside it.

Data

Users
300 synthetic users × 36 months.
Transactions
About 381,000, spanning Oct 2023 to Sep 2026.
Variety
Four user types, German merchants, seasonality and shocks.
Testing
289 tests at 92% coverage.

Approach

  • Lag, rolling and calendar features per category family.
  • Three baselines to beat: last month, same month last year, and a 3-month average.
  • A global LightGBM model per family, with ETS and SARIMAX as comparisons, tuned with Optuna.
  • A model becomes champion only if it beats the best baseline by at least 10%.
  • Categoriser: keyword rules first, then TF-IDF with logistic regression.
  • Rolling-origin backtesting over 23 months, plus anomaly detection on transactions.

Results

Spending MAE in EUR over a 23-month backtest — lower is better

Best baseline — MAE59.33
LightGBM — MAE55.23

That is a 6.9% improvement. Gains against the best baseline vary by category:

Improvement over best baseline, %

Health23%
Leisure19%
Transport15%
Income11%
Interval coverage
Ranges contain the real value 85% of the time for spending, 78% for income.
Categoriser
100% on held-out users; 91% on unseen merchants for confident predictions.
Anomalies
88% of planted anomalies caught, at 49% precision.

App pages: Home, Overview, Import, Forecast, What-If, Anomalies, Goals, Profile.

Limitations
& honest notes

  • Synthetic data only. Real spending is messier than any generator.
  • Freelancer income is hard to forecast — income intervals cover 78% against 85% for spending.
  • One-month horizon only.
  • Short histories raise error noticeably.
  • Anomaly precision is 49%, so roughly half of what is flagged is not a real anomaly.
  • Not financial advice.
